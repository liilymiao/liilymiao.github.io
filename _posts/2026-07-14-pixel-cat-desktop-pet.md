To all wizards, Muggles, researchers, programmers, and everyone whose desktop has already disappeared beneath documents, code, spreadsheets, and meetings:

Today, this establishment is releasing its latest invention—a fully domesticated pixel cat capable of living independently on your computer desktop.

Its purpose is perfectly clear: to offer quiet and suspicious companionship.

When nobody is watching, it sits peacefully with its phone, entirely uninterested in the mountain of work around it. The moment your cursor approaches, however, it puts the phone away, produces a tiny computer, and starts typing at astonishing speed, wearing the unmistakable expression of someone who has been working intensely all along.

We make no guarantees regarding its professional ethics, actual output, or annual performance review. It has, however, mastered one of the oldest and most practical forms of office magic: looking extremely busy whenever someone is watching.

This cat began life inside a Codex project. After several rounds of domestication—including window creation, frame-by-frame animation, state switching, cursor response, drag interaction, context menus, application signing, packaging, and archive verification—it now has standalone macOS App and Windows `.exe` editions, and will not suddenly demand a premium cat-food subscription.

It has left the development environment and can now be summoned directly onto an ordinary computer.

This establishment will not accept complaints concerning short working hours, excessive phone use, sudden displays of busyness when the cursor passes by, an insistence on resuming its original slacking pose after being dragged, or lost productivity caused by watching the pixel cat. All of the above are intended design features.

You are welcome to take it home to your computer.

Whether it is simply occupying the best spot on the desktop is a question this establishment declines to answer.
